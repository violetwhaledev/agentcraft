P5 Make proving-it-yourself part of saying 'done' Falsifiable acceptance BUILD THIS HABIT
WEAK In several episodes you called the work finished without personally watching it pass a real check -- falsifiable_acceptance sits at 4/6. The instinct to verify is there; the reflex of 'I don't say done until I've seen it go green' isn't yet automatic in you.
WHY Proving-it-yourself-before-you-say-done is what makes your word trustworthy -- to a reviewer, to your next session, and to you. It transfers everywhere: the moment 'done' means 'I watched it pass', regressions stop sneaking back and you stop re-discovering the same bug next session. This is prove-don't-declare, lived out with your own hands.
HOW Make it a personal rule you feel: you don't type 'done' until YOU have watched the real check go green in that same sitting. Your next 10 completions, run the pass/fail gate yourself and read the exit line before you claim it works. If you can't prove it right now, say 'not verified yet' honestly instead of 'done'.
PRACTICE — try this yourself
Next time you finish a change, before you write 'done', ask yourself: 'Did I personally watch this pass? No? Then I run it now and read the green line first.' -- then claim it.

P6 Build the habit of starting from what you already know BUILD THIS HABIT
WEAK You carry decisions forward only some of the time -- cross_session_inheritance and mistake_nonrepetition both sit at 2/4. When you begin a new session, you don't yet reliably pause to recall what past-you already figured out, so you sometimes re-derive it from scratch.
WHY Starting from what you already decided is the continuity muscle that compounds -- it's the single habit that turns a string of sessions into one growing body of work, and it transfers to every project and every tool you'll use. Build it in yourself and you stop re-solving solved problems and stop repeating a mistake you already paid for once. Every session that starts blind is a session that pays the same tuition twice.
HOW Make it a personal opening ritual: before your first real task in any new session, take 60 seconds to ask yourself 'what did I already decide, and what did I already learn the hard way here?' -- and go read your own last handoff or note before you touch anything. Your next 5 sessions, open with that recall, in your own hands, so the first thing you do is inherit, not restart.
PRACTICE — try this yourself
At the start of your next session, before the first task, say to yourself: 'What did last-me leave me? Read the last handoff first.' -- then summarize the one or two decisions you're inheriting in a sentence before you begin.
↻ track in yourself next scan: Next scan, watch your own cross_session_inheritance -- aim to feel yourself instinctively reach for last session's decisions before starting fresh. You'll notice the ritual become automatic before the number climbs.
